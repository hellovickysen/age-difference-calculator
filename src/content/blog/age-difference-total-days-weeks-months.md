The same age gap can be shown in different ways. You may see years, months and days, total days, or weeks and days.

[Use the calculator](/#calculator) to see these answers together.

## Total days

Total days count every full day that passed between the two dates. Leap days are included automatically.

This is useful when you want one clear number.

For example, from 1 April to 11 April, ten full days pass. The starting date is not counted as a completed day. This is an elapsed-time answer, which may differ from a list that counts both calendar dates.

## Weeks and days

One week has seven days. To show weeks, divide the total days by seven.

For example, 24 days equals 3 weeks and 3 days.

The week answer is easy to read, but it is still based on the total-day count.

If the total is 100 days, the result is 14 full weeks and 2 days because 14 × 7 is 98.

## Total months need real dates

Months do not have one fixed length. A month can have 28, 29, 30 or 31 days.

Because of this, you should not divide total days by 30 when you need an exact month answer. Count full calendar months from the starting date instead.

For example, 1 February to 1 March is one calendar month. It may contain 28 or 29 days. The month answer stays one, while the total-day answer depends on the year.

## Worked comparison

Compare 5 January 2000 with 20 March 2001.

The calendar result is **1 year, 2 months and 15 days**. The same period can also be shown as one total number of days, then divided into full weeks and days.

The calendar result is usually easier to explain as an age gap. The total-day result is better for calculations that require one fixed unit.

## What about hours and minutes?

A date-only calculator starts each date at the same point in the day. It does not know the birth time.

For an exact difference in hours or minutes, you need both birth times and the correct time zones.

Multiplying total days by 24 gives date-based hours only when both dates use the same assumed start time. It does not recover unknown birth times.

## Avoid conversion mistakes

- Do not multiply years by 365 when the period may include leap days.
- Do not multiply months by 30 for an exact answer.
- Do not call a decimal year result an exact calendar age.
- Check whether “days between” means elapsed days or inclusive date counting.
- Use the same date format for both entries.

## Which result is best?

- Use years, months and days for an easy age description.
- Use total days when you need one exact number.
- Use weeks for short gaps.
- Use birth times only when hours and minutes truly matter.

Choose the answer that matches your question.

If you are comparing two people, start with years, months and days. Add total days only when it helps the reader or the form specifically asks for it.
