In most cases, **age gap** and **age difference** mean the same thing. Both describe the time between two dates of birth.

You can use the [Age Difference Calculator](/#calculator) for either question.

## When people say “age gap”

People often use “age gap” when talking about family or relationships.

Examples:

- The age gap between two brothers
- The age gap between husband and wife
- The age gap between a parent and child

In normal conversation, people may round the answer. They may say “about five years” even when the exact gap includes extra months and days.

## When people say “age difference”

“Age difference” is common when someone wants a clear calculation. A calculator may show the answer as years, months and days.

For example:

> 5 years, 3 months and 8 days

This is more exact than saying “about five years.”

## Which phrase should you use?

Use the phrase that feels natural for your question. The calculation stays the same.

- For daily conversation, “age gap” is simple and common.
- For an exact result, “age difference” may be clearer.

## What the number cannot tell you

An age-gap number cannot tell you whether a relationship is good or bad. It only compares two dates. Personal choices, laws and family matters need more information than a calculator can give.
