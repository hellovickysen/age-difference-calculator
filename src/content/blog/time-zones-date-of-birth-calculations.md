For most age calculations, you enter only the day, month and year. In this case, a time zone should not change the date you entered.

## A date and a time are different

“10 May 2000” is a calendar date.

“10 May 2000 at 11:30 PM in Kolkata” is a date, time and place. It gives much more detail.

The [Age Difference Calculator](/#calculator) uses dates only. It does not ask for birth times or places.

## How a one-day error can happen

Some computer systems turn every date into a time. They may then change that time to another time zone.

A late-night time in one country can become the next day in another country. If the system is not careful, the birth date may move by one day.

## How this calculator handles dates

The calculator keeps the day, month and year as entered. It does not need to move a birth date from India to another time zone.

## When time zones really matter

Time zones matter when you compare exact birth times, such as:

- Twins born minutes apart
- Babies born in different countries
- Events recorded with exact times
- Records close to midnight

For these cases, you need the correct local time, place and time zone for each record.

## Simple rule

Use dates for an age gap in years, months and days. Use times and time zones only when you need an answer in hours or minutes.
