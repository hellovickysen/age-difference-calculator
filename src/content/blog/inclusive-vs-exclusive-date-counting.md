Two people can get different answers between the same dates because they are counting in different ways.

One method measures time passed. Another method counts every date shown on the calendar.

## Elapsed days

Elapsed days measure how much time passed after the starting date.

From 1 June to 2 June, one day has passed. This method does not count 1 June as a full day inside the answer.

The [Age Difference Calculator](/#calculator) uses elapsed time because it measures the gap between two birth dates.

## Counting all dates

Sometimes people want to count every date in a range, including the first and last date.

From 1 June to 2 June, the dates are:

1. 1 June
2. 2 June

So the inclusive count is two calendar dates, even though only one day passed.

## Which answer should you use?

Use elapsed days for:

- Age difference
- Travel time
- Time between two events
- Number of days passed

Count all dates when instructions clearly say that both the start and end dates are included.

## Read the label carefully

Look for words such as “days apart”, “days passed”, “including both dates” or “count every day”. These words tell you which method is needed.

## Simple example

From 10 July to 15 July:

- Elapsed time is 5 days.
- Counting both calendar dates gives 6 dates.

Neither answer is always wrong. They answer different questions.
